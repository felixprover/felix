# Public notation and ontology

This index records the main notation used by maintained library source. The
“kind” column is part of the interface: two expressions that render similarly
may inhabit different logical or set-theoretic levels. Technical helper names
are documented at their definitions and in `lexicon.tsv`; entries below
cover the notation intended for ordinary mathematical statements.

## Logical framework

| Source notation | Reading | Kind | Home |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `\Set` | the type of sets | concrete logical type | Felix logical syntax |
| `\Prop` | the type of propositions | concrete logical type | Felix logical syntax |
| `\tau\to\sigma` | functions from `\tau` to `\sigma` | concrete logical function type; right-associative | Felix logical syntax |
| `\fun{x:\tau}{t}` | the function taking `x` to `t` | logical lambda term | Felix logical syntax |
| `\Apply{F}{x}` or `\Apply{F}{x_1,\ldots,x_n}` | logical application of `F` to one or more arguments, applied left-to-right | logical term | Felix logical syntax |
| `\forall x:\tau.\ P` and `\exists x:\tau.\ P` | typed quantification | logical binder | Felix logical syntax |
| `\Select{P}` or `\Select[\tau]{P}` | an object satisfying `P`, when one exists | typed logical selection term | Felix logical syntax and `logic/conditional.tex` |
| `\sep{A}{P}` | elements of `A` satisfying logical predicate `P` | set | checked prelude interface |
| `\lfp{D}{F}` | least fixed point of logical operator `F` in `D` | set | checked prelude interface |

Logical application is never written as ordinary `f(x)`. The latter is
reserved for a set-coded function graph.

Commas at the outer `\Apply` level always separate its curried arguments. A
formula argument containing a comma-separated relation chain must therefore
be parenthesized.

The witness law for `\choice{A}` requires that `A` be inhabited. No value for
the empty set is exposed. This operator is not `\Select`. Function application
is totalized, so inverse laws must not be inferred outside the declared graph
domains. Writing `f(x)` itself has no formation requirement. When an enclosing
typed term requests a classification of the result, checked notion rules may
derive `f(x)\in B` from `f:A\to B` and `x\in A`; that evidence remains local to
the formation query. When `f:A\to B` and `g:B\to A` are known, the typed
inverse theorems reduce the graph-level contracts to the usual pointwise
equations on `A` or `B`.

For notion reasoning, `f\in\funs{A}{B}` and “`f` is a function from `A` to
`B`” are definitional synonyms. Either spelling supplies the same exact
function-space evidence, including domain and application-result inference;
the library may use whichever is clearer in context.

## Orders, ordinals, and cardinals

Primitive laws such as reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity remain useful
raw predicates on arbitrary sets. Public composite structures—quasiorders,
orders, strict orders, equivalence relations, and well-founded relations—also
assert that their relation argument is a relation.

## Topology, metrics, and uniformity

Raw coordinate charts are injective graphs on a bare source set with open
image in a displayed model. `\chartTransition{d}{c}` is the graph
`d\circ\converse{c}`, with its natural overlap domain; the target chart comes
first. Its home is `topology/coordinate-chart.tex`.
`\coordinateOpens{X}{E}{A}` consists of the subsets of `X` with open image
under every chart in `A`; graph images already take the trace on each chart
domain. Its home is `topology/coordinate-topology.tex`.
`\CoordinateTopology{X}{E}{A}` packages this topology with carrier `X`.

A local-map datum stores `\models[C]` and `\admissible[C]`, without a carrier.
A local-map calculus imposes endpoint soundness and the identity, restriction,
composition, and locality laws. “`f` is admissible from `E` to `F` in `C`”
is the public interface to its stored relation. `\ContinuousCalculus{M}` is
the exact calculus of continuous partial maps on the topological model family
`M`. Its set bound is `\modelCarrierUnion{M}`, the union of the model carriers;
`\continuousAdmissibility{M}` collects the endpoint-labelled graphs within
that bound. These interfaces live in `topology/local-map-calculus.tex` and
`topology/continuous-calculus.tex`.

For a model `E` of a local-map calculus `C`,
`\DiffeomorphismPseudogroup{C}{E}` is the exact coordinate pseudogroup whose
changes are admissible in both directions. The bounded set
`\invertibleChanges{C}{E}` contains precisely these open partial
homeomorphisms. “Invertibly admissible from `E` to `F` in `C`” also applies
between different models. These interfaces live in
`topology/calculus-pseudogroup.tex`.

`\PseudogroupIntersection{E}{P}` intersects a set-sized pseudogroup family `P`
on the fixed model `E`. Its changes, `\commonCoordinateChanges{E}{P}`, are
bounded by all open partial homeomorphisms of `E`. The empty family therefore
gives `\FullPseudogroup{E}`. The home is
`topology/pseudogroup-intersection.tex`.

“`A` presents `S` on `X` under `G`” says that `A` is an atlas on the fixed
space and generates `atlas[S]`. `\ChartsAt{S}{p}` is the bounded set of stored
charts whose domains contain `p`; it applies to relative and equipped records
and stores no choice of chart. The home is
`topology/coordinate-atlas-presentation.tex`.

`\RestrictedAtlas{A}{U}` in `topology/coordinate-atlas-restriction.tex` is the
set of graphs obtained by restricting each member of A to U. For an atlas and
an ambient open U, it is an atlas on the open subspace; maximality and the
presentation-independence laws require their stated hypotheses.

`\TransportedAtlas{A}{h}` in `topology/coordinate-transport.tex` is the set
of graphs `c ∘ converse(h)` for `c` in A. For a homeomorphism from X to Y,
it transports an atlas on X to Y without changing its model or pseudogroup.
The set-valued operation is defined without domain hypotheses; its identity
and inverse laws require the stated graph and domain conditions.

`\RestrictedCoordinates{G}{X}{S}{U}` in `topology/coordinate-restriction.tex`
is the exact relative coordinate structure on the open subspace of X at U,
under G. Its atlas is `\RestrictedAtlas{\atlas[S]}{U}`; its hypotheses require
S to be a relative coordinate structure on X under G and U to be open in X.
The equipped form is the existing `Equip` construction on that subspace.

`\TransportedCoordinates{G}{X}{Y}{S}{h}` in `topology/coordinate-homeomorphism.tex`
is the exact relative coordinate structure on Y under G obtained from S on X
through a homeomorphism h from X to Y. Its atlas is
`\TransportedAtlas{\atlas[S]}{h}`. Equipping uses `Equip` with Y, retaining
the exact topological reduct of Y rather than identifying a richer record
with its reduct.

`\WeakenedCoordinates{G}{H}{X}{S}` in `topology/coordinate-weakening.tex`
is the exact relative coordinate structure on X under H generated by the
atlas of S. Its hypotheses require S to be relative coordinates on X under G
and G to be a subpseudogroup of H. The atlas is maximalized under H; the
topology is unchanged. Any G-presentation of S computes this same structure
by generation under H.

`\CoordExpr{d}{f}{c}{U}` is
`(d|f[U]) ∘ ((f|U) ∘ converse(c|U))`. The target chart comes first, as with
`\chartTransition{d}{c}`. The target restriction is a graph operation and
does not assert that `f[U]` is open. Under the endpoint and chart-domain
conditions, its exact domain is `c[U]`. The home is
`topology/coordinate-expression.tex`.

“`f` is coordinate-admissible from `M` under `G` to `N` under `H` in `C`”
uses equipped endpoint spaces, explicit coordinate pseudogroups, and a local-map
calculus. It requires endpoint typing, an open exact domain, and admissible
coordinate expressions locally. The point-local form inserts “at `p`” after
“coordinate-admissible”. Neither form asserts that the map has open image.
The home is `topology/coordinate-map.tex`.

The adjective “compact” is defined directly for topological spaces.  Its
declaration header supplies both the logical topological-space conjunct and
the source-level notion requirement; no separate “compact space” wrapper is
used.  The older raw/typed distinction remains for “connected”, “normal”, and
their corresponding space phrases until those APIs are reviewed separately.
An “almost-disjoint family” may be empty or finite, although each member is
infinite. The existing adjective in “infinite almost-disjoint family” requires
the collection itself to be infinite. Maximal almost-disjoint families include
finite examples such as `\{\naturals\}`; the term “MAD family” is reserved here
for an infinite maximal almost-disjoint family.
The opt-in `structure/finite.tex` module similarly defines the structural
reading of “finite” on one-sorted structures by finiteness of their carriers.
Complete phrases select this separately from ordinary set finiteness; a bare
use is ambiguous when both readings are visible.
Unlike raw space adjectives, the phrase “`A` is closed in `X`” includes the
mathematical requirement `A\subseteq\carrier[X]`. Closure ignores elements of
`A` outside the carrier, equivalently taking the closure of its carrier trace.
